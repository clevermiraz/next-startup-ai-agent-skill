# Research Notes: Human Buying Psychology

This file supports the `human-buying-psychology` skill. It is a starting bibliography, not an exhaustive literature review. Check original publications and current local law before relying on a claim in a commercial or regulated setting.

## Consumer decision journeys

**Santos, S. & Gonçalves, H. M. (2021).** "The consumer decision journey: A literature review of foundational models and theories and a future perspective." *Technological Forecasting and Social Change*, 173, 121117.

- Useful for understanding the multi-stage nature of consumer decision-making.
- The journey should be treated as a flexible model; real decisions can loop and revisit stages.
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2021.121117

## Consumer information processing and behavioral decision research

**Schmitt, B. (2024).** "Consumer information processing and decision-making: Origins, findings, applications, and future directions." *Journal of Consumer Research*, 51(1), 2–6.

- Provides an overview of consumer information processing and behavioral decision research.
- Use it as a field overview rather than evidence that any one marketing tactic will work.
- Link: https://academic.oup.com/jcr

**Kahneman, D. (2011).** *Thinking, Fast and Slow.* Farrar, Straus and Giroux.

- Popular synthesis of dual-process ideas, heuristics, and judgment.
- Do not interpret "System 1" and "System 2" as two literal brain modules or as a complete account of every decision.

**Tversky, A. & Kahneman, D. (1974).** "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases." *Science*, 185(4157), 1124–1131.

- Foundational account of heuristics and biases in judgment.
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124

**Thaler, R. H. & Sunstein, C. R. (2008).** *Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness.* Yale University Press.

- Introduces choice architecture and nudges.
- Apply defaults and choice design transparently, with easy opt-out and customer welfare in view.

## Trust and purchase intention

**Wang, J., Shahzad, F., Ahmad, Z., Abdullah, M., & Hassan, N. M. (2022).** "Trust and Consumers' Purchase Intention in a Social Commerce Platform: A Meta-Analytic Approach." *SAGE Open*, 12(2).

- Meta-analytic evidence reports a positive relationship between trust and purchase intention in social commerce settings.
- Avoid assuming the same effect size applies to every product category or market.
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440221091262

## Motivation and emotion

**Rutgers University, Consumer Analysis: Motivation, Emotion, and Involvement (lecture notes).**

- Introductory definitions of consumer motivation and emotional goals.
- Educational notes are useful for orientation but should not replace peer-reviewed primary research.
- Link: https://crab.rutgers.edu/users/ckaufman/ConsumerAnalysismotivationnotes.html

## Ethical marketing

**American Marketing Association.** *AMA Statement of Ethics.*

- Professional guidance emphasizing honesty, responsibility, fairness, transparency, and respect.
- Use alongside applicable consumer-protection, advertising, privacy, and subscription laws in the target jurisdiction.
- Link: https://www.ama.org/ama-statement-of-ethics/

## Cross-cultural and international trust

**Dong, Y. (2026).** "Navigating trust in cross-border e-commerce: a systematic review of cultural and consumer dynamics." *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*, 13, 237.

- Reviews how cultural and cross-border context can affect trust formation.
- Treat group-level findings cautiously; never infer an individual's preferences from a national or cultural label.
- Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-026-06579-4

## How to use this bibliography

When an agent needs to make a specific empirical claim:

1. Locate the original paper or authoritative publication.
2. Confirm the exact claim, sample, method, and date.
3. Check whether later reviews or replications qualify the finding.
4. State limitations and avoid universal language.
5. Prefer first-party customer research when deciding what a particular customer segment values.
